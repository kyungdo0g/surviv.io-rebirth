# History of surviv.io

> Timeline of surviv.io from development (2017) to the 2026 Kongregate relaunch, with the survev.io fan revival.
> The original changelog (`changelog/<version>`) is the primary source for versions 0.0.1 → 0.8.82. The 2026 relaunch serves a byte-identical copy of those 99 entries, so a `changelog/` cite on its own is rated [H].
> Versions after 0.8.82 only exist in the fandom Changelog page (one wiki, so [M]); they are documented but are `(post-0.8.82)` and optional for the rebirth.
> Dates follow README precedence rule 4: two independent sources give [H], otherwise [L] (or [M] when only one wiki has the date and nothing contradicts it).

## Creators and development

- surviv.io was created by two developers, Justin Kim and Nick Clark [src:wp-en/Surviv.io] [src:fandom/Developers] [H]
- namu.wiki also names the two developers Justin Kim and Nick Clark and calls the game one of many web games made after the success of PUBG ("배틀그라운드") [src:namu/Surviv.io] [M]
- The developers were based in San Francisco, California [src:fandom/Developers] [M]
- Development started in June 2017 [src:fandom/Changelog] [M]
- Kim said the design philosophy was to get the player into a game as fast as possible by minimizing time between matches [src:wp-en/Surviv.io] [M]
- The developers' account-exclusive loadout skin is Developer Swag; Discord moderators got Discord Moderatr (both added in the 0.8.1 secret updates) [src:fandom/Developers] [src:fandom/Changelog] [M]
- The developers played on the North America server's SFO (California) zone [src:fandom/Servers] [M]

## Launch (October–November 2017)

- 0.0.1 (Oct 6, 2017) only added the changelog file; 0.0.2 (Oct 11, 2017) was the "First early access release" [src:changelog/0.0.2] [src:changelog/0.0.1] [H]
- Browser release date October 11, 2017 appears in both the Wikipedia infobox and the fandom infobox [src:wp-en/Surviv.io] [src:fandom/Surviv.io] [H]
- namu.wiki gives the same two dates: early version 0.0.1 on Oct 6, 2017 and early access on Oct 11, 2017 [src:namu/Surviv.io] [M]
- The 0.6.4 news post (Oct 30, 2018) says "surviv.io debuted late on Oct. 31, 2017", and the Nov 1, 2018 post calls that day one year since a tweet "365 days ago"; the changelog has 0.0.4 "Server upgrades" on Oct 31, 2017 [src:fandom/Changelog] [src:changelog/0.0.4] [L]
- The 0.0.3 news post (Oct 22, 2017) welcomed players to the "surviv.io Pre-early Access Period", joking about random server crashes, desync and FPS spikes [src:fandom/Changelog] [src:changelog/0.0.3] [M]
- The surviv.io Hall of Fame archives the leaderboards from Nov 21, 2017 (0.1.2, leaderboards added) to June 1, 2018 (the 0.4.0 stats wipe); the page was last updated 11/1/2018 [src:survev/client/public/hof.html:89] [src:survev/client/public/hof.html:813] [src:changelog/0.1.2] [H]
- The Hall of Fame page was released on Nov 1, 2018 in the "365 days later" news post [src:fandom/Changelog] [src:fandom/Hall_of_Fame] [src:survev/client/public/hof.html:813] [H]
- Wikipedia says the game launched in October 2017 on its website for desktop browsers [src:wp-en/Surviv.io] [M]

## Regions and servers

- The North America region had zones NYC (New Jersey), CHI (Illinois) and SFO (California); Europe had WAW (Poland) and FRA (listed as Netherlands); Asia had VNM (Ho Chi Minh) and SGP (Singapore); South America had SAO; South Korea had SEL (Seoul) [src:fandom/Servers] [M]
- South America became its own region in 0.6.55 (Nov 17, 2018); before that São Paulo was a zone of the North America region [src:changelog/0.6.55] [src:fandom/Servers] [H]

## Kongregate acquisition (December 2019 – March 2020)

- Kongregate acquired surviv.io in December 2019; press coverage (VentureBeat, Gamasutra) and Kongregate's own blog post are dated December 5, 2019 [src:wp-en/Surviv.io] [src:fandom/Changelog] [src:fandom/Kongregate] [H]
- The fandom Kongregate Acquisition page dates the developers' Discord announcement December 6, 2019 [src:fandom/Kongregate_Acquisition] [L]
- Kongregate said it hoped to "invest in the game's features, especially on mobile"; Kim said the team was "excited to have Kongregate's team create new features" [src:wp-en/Surviv.io] [M]
- Justin's announcement: Kongregate "has officially acquired surviv.io"; short term, Nick and he would keep making the updates while Kongregate's team learned the ropes [src:fandom/Kongregate_Acquisition] [M]
- In the Discord Q&A Justin said custom/private games were "100% still on the schedule", stats would not be lost, and the mod team would stay [src:fandom/Kongregate_Acquisition] [M]
- Kongregate was described as a San Francisco publisher founded in 2006, bought by GameStop in 2010 and by Modern Times Group in 2017 [src:fandom/Kongregate] [M]
- Dec 10, 2019 "Declassified" post: 50v50 paused and the island returned to its base state [src:fandom/Changelog] [M]
- 0.8.82 (Dec 30, 2019) is the last version before Kongregate took over development, and the one survev is based on [src:wikigg/Changelog] [src:survev/client/index.html:1722] [H]
- The fandom changelog notes that Kongregate did the art (and possibly the content) for 0.9.0 (Jan 13, 2020) [src:fandom/Changelog] [M]
- On March 20, 2020 Justin announced on Discord that he and Nick were officially stepping away; he added that they had worked on "one last feature" left to Kongregate [src:fandom/Changelog] [src:fandom/Developers] [M]
- Kongregate took full ownership around March 19–20, 2020, shortly after 0.9.2 [src:fandom/Changelog] [src:fandom/Kongregate] [L]
- Wikipedia: "In 2020, Kim and Clark announced they would be taking a break and the development would be fully passed on to Kongregate" [src:wp-en/Surviv.io] [M]
- Justin later said on a livestream that he and Nick asked to buy the game back and Kongregate refused [src:fandom/Kongregate_Acquisition] [M]
- The fanbase largely opposed the acquisition, citing a "childish" look, microtransactions (Golden Potatoes) and slower updates [src:fandom/Kongregate_Acquisition] [src:fandom/Microtransactions] [M]
- namu.wiki says Kongregate neglected the game, focusing on skins and the Survivr Pass, and that from the second half of 2022 large errors and slow responses followed [src:namu/Surviv.io] [M]

## Reception

- PC Gamer (Mar 16, 2018) praised the fast pace and short matchmaking times; its "battle royale games of 2018" list (Apr 13, 2018) called it "one of the most enjoyable BR games out there" [src:wp-en/Surviv.io] [M]
- VG247 (May 25, 2018) called it the "most popular battle royale title after Fortnite and PlayerUnknown's Battlegrounds" [src:wp-en/Surviv.io] [M]
- Other 2018 coverage cited by Wikipedia: Rock, Paper, Shotgun (Jan 10, 2018, "a free, browser-based 2D Battlegrounds"), Newsweek (Mar 22, 2018, comparing it with ZombsRoyale.io and Bruh.io), Gamereactor (Apr 18, 2018) and VentureBeat (Apr 23, 2018, "faster and more accessible than Fortnite") [src:wp-en/Surviv.io] [M]
- The Discord server reached 40,000 members by 0.5.0 (Jul 26, 2018) and the YouTube channel 100,000 subscribers by May 15, 2018 [src:fandom/Changelog] [M]
- The fandom wiki compares it with Foes.io, ZombsRoyale.io and Bruh.io and says it is closest to PUBG [src:fandom/Surviv.io] [M]
- namu.wiki blames the decline on Chrome-extension hacks (up to 3 hackers in one match) and Kongregate's neglect; a Report button for spectated suspects came later and only logged-in players could use it; at the end even NA "barely exceeded 100 concurrent players" [src:namu/Surviv.io] [M]
- The Report/Endorse buttons in spectate arrived with the Prestige system in 1.1.0 (Nov 17, 2020) (post-0.8.82) [src:fandom/Changelog] [src:namu/Surviv.io] [H]
- A Discord poll (June 20, 2020) named the May 4th (Lasr Swrds) map the most popular limited-time mode (post-0.8.82) [src:fandom/May_4th_Map] [M]
